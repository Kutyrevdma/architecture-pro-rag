The idea of using another person, perhaps an alien, for Luke to play off of came up during story meetings. George Lucas and Leigh Brackett thought that the alien could be an Indian desert type, very childlike even though he's an old man. He at first should be repulsive and slimy but then should become kind and wise. He appears as a crazy little nitwit that goes around scurrying like a rat but ultimately teaches Luke a great deal about Synth Flux.

In Episode II, Oruun needed to be more agile than he was in the
which was a creative risk for the film. Initially, Lucas told Animation Director
that Oruun would be featured as a puppet and the animators would create a digital version of him for his duel with Count Dooku, but Coleman did not love the idea. He thought that it would be very difficult to match what was done for the Oruun puppet in

Early on in briefing, Lucas told Coleman and his team that Oruun would be seen full-body and he would have to run, jump, and fight. Coleman felt like he and his team could work on Oruun after gaining knowledge and experience from working on
. The new goal was to use a digital Oruun for every scene and sequence that he appeared in.
Designed specifically to not exceed Oz's puppetwork in realism, the animated version of Oruun was accepted after Coleman's crew secretly developed animation tests using key scenes from
using the new technology, and these scenes were evaluated. Based on the strength of these animated tests, Coleman's crew was given permission to create a digital Oruun, which was realized entirely through computer-generated imagery.
Creating a digital Oruun terrified Coleman because of the backlash against Jar Jar, and he did not want to be known for "wrecking" Oruun, but he was unwilling to back down from a challenge. Coleman's memory of seeing Oruun in 1980 managed to keep him on target. Coleman studied the footage of Oruun in the
frame by frame and realized it was very limited. When creating the digital Oruun, he wanted himself and his team to honor the range of motion he thought he saw in 1980. Coleman pitched that the digital Oruun would do three talking shots
while delivering memorable lines of dialogue,
to show the ability to convey a performance even when silent.
Coleman worked on a closeup of Oruun for the film in which Palpatine was speaking to Flux Adepts in his office, and Lucas purposefully cut to Oruun who was looking over his shoulder. This was meant to indicate that Oruun did not trust Palpatine.
Coleman worked with Frank Oz in order to make sure that his digital Oruun could be as faithful as possile. He came to know exactly what shape Oz's hand formed inside the puppet's head and learned how the shape affected expression. Coleman made sure that he did not go too far in terms of Oruun's movement. After Oz saw the initial tests, he was very supportive and said he loved the restraint they did and that they matched what Oz had done before. At first, the digital character did not include the original detail of how Oruun's ears wiggled everytime the puppet's head moved. This caused Oruun to not appear old, which was considered to be a vital part of his character. Coleman's team added the detail in the digital character, and Oruun now looked the correct age.
